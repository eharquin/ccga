\begin{abstract}
We present Conic Conformal Geometric Algebra (CCGA), the geometric algebra
$\mathbb{R}_{5,3}$ for the geometry of planar conics, as a systematic
foundation for two-dimensional conic geometry. CCGA renames and consolidates
the algebra previously introduced as QC2GA and proven isomorphic to the
Geometric Algebra for Conics (GAC). We give the complete catalogue of geometric objects in
both outer-product null space (OPNS) and inner-product null space (IPNS) form,
with their grades and reality conditions; the join, meet, and dual operations;
closed-form, geometric-algebra-native extraction of every classical conic
feature directly from the dual conic vector; the versor transformation group
together with a precise account of which planar maps it realises; and a unified
treatment of conic intersection based on a shared-factor reduction of the
regressive product. Every construction is given in closed geometric-algebra form
with its reality conditions.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Geometric Algebra (GA), also known as Clifford algebra, provides a unified
language for geometry in which objects and the transformations acting on them
live in a single associative algebra~\cite{dorst_geometric_2010,
perwass_geometric_2009}. Its conformal model represents points as null
vectors, circles and lines as blades, and conformal maps as versors acting by
the sandwich product --- a formulation that has proven fruitful in classical
mechanics and physics~\cite{hestenes_new_2012,hestenes_zitterbewegung_1990,
gregory_elastic_2017}, in computer graphics~\cite{de_keninck_non-parametric_2019},
and more recently in geometric deep learning~\cite{buchholz_optimal_2007,
ruhe_geometric_2023}. For the Euclidean plane, however, the conformal model
stops at circles and lines: the general conic --- ellipse, parabola,
hyperbola, and their degenerate limits --- is outside its reach, even though
conics are the natural next family of planar curves in vision, graphics, and
kinematics.

Several algebras have been proposed to close this gap. Perwass embedded plane
conics in $\mathbb{R}_{5,3}$ via a Veronese-type point map as a proof of
concept~\cite{perwass_geometric_2009}; Easter and Hitzer's Double Conformal
Geometric Algebra~\cite{easter_double_2017} and Goldman and Mann's
$\mathbb{R}_{4,4}$ framework~\cite{goldman_r4_2015} capture wider quadric
families at the price of losing some operations; Breuils et al.\ introduced
Quadric Conformal Geometric Algebra (QCGA, $\mathbb{R}_{9,6}$) for
three-dimensional quadrics~\cite{breuils2018quadric,
breuils_three-dimensional_2019}, whose two-dimensional restriction QC2GA is
again $\mathbb{R}_{5,3}$; and Hrdina, N\'avrat, and Va\v{s}\'ik proposed the
Geometric Algebra for Conics (GAC)~\cite{hrdina_geometric_2018}, since used
for conic fitting~\cite{2024-01-09louckaAlgorithmsConicFitting} and for conic
intersection in special configurations~\cite{byrtus_specific_2021}. QC2GA and
GAC were recently proven isomorphic --- the same algebra in two
bases~\cite{chomicki_intersection_2023}. Each of these contributions, however,
introduced its own basis, normalisation, and name, and each developed only the
slice of the theory its application required: the object catalogue, the
feature-extraction formulas, the transformation group, and the intersection
theory have never been assembled in one place, in one notation.

This paper provides that consolidation. We adopt the name \emph{Conic
Conformal Geometric Algebra} (CCGA) for $\mathbb{R}_{5,3}$ equipped with the
null working basis and point embedding of \cref{sec:algebra,sec:point}, and we
develop the algebra as a self-contained foundation: the complete taxonomy of
its objects in both OPNS and IPNS form, organised by a single construction
ladder; closed-form extraction of every classical conic feature directly from
the dual conic vector by inner products with a fixed set of special blades;
a precise characterisation of which planar maps the versor group realises; and
a unified treatment of conic intersection by grade arithmetic of the
regressive product. Throughout, a single design principle is enforced:
\emph{every structural operation is geometric algebra; the only numeric
residue is root extraction} (square roots and polynomial roots, which carry
the irreducible algebraic content of intersecting curves).

\subsection{Contributions}
\label{sec:contributions}
The main contributions of this paper are:
\begin{itemize}
  \item A self-contained construction of \textbf{Conic Conformal Geometric
    Algebra} (CCGA, $\mathbb{R}_{5,3}$), unifying the former QC2GA and GAC
    under one name and one notation (\cref{sec:algebra,sec:point}).
  \item The \textbf{complete object taxonomy} of CCGA in both OPNS and IPNS
    form, with grades, geometric meaning, and reality conditions, organised by
    a single construction ladder (\cref{sec:conics,sec:taxonomy}).
  \item \textbf{Geometric-algebra-native feature extraction}: closed-form
    formulas reading the type, center, axes, foci, eccentricity, asymptotic
    directions, and tangent/polar lines of a conic directly from its dual
    grade-$1$ vector by inner products (\cref{sec:extraction}).
  \item A precise characterisation of the \textbf{versor transformation group}
    of CCGA — which planar maps are sandwich versors (similarities) and which
    are only linear outermorphisms (shears, non-uniform scalings, projective
    maps) (\cref{sec:transforms}).
  \item A unified \textbf{intersection framework} based on a shared-factor /
    B\'ezout reduction of the regressive product, complementing the pencil
    method of~\cite{chomicki_intersection_2023} (\cref{sec:intersections}).
\end{itemize}

\section{The CCGA Algebra \texorpdfstring{$\mathbb{R}_{5,3}$}{R(5,3)}}
\label{sec:algebra}
CCGA is the geometric algebra $\mathbb{R}_{5,3}$ (five positive, three negative,
no null basis axes), of dimension $2^8 = 256$. It is the conic algebra first
introduced by Perwass~\cite{perwass_geometric_2009}, developed as the
two-dimensional restriction QC2GA of Quadric Conformal Geometric
Algebra~\cite{breuils_three-dimensional_2019} and shown isomorphic to the
Geometric Algebra for Conics (GAC)~\cite{hrdina_geometric_2018}; we call it Conic
Conformal Geometric Algebra and relate the two bases in
\cref{sec:gac}. Because the metric is \emph{non-degenerate}, the pseudoscalar is
invertible, so the dual, meet, and join are all clean --- in contrast with the
degenerate metric of PGA, where these require special care.

\subsection{Diagonal and null bases}
The orthogonal basis splits into the Euclidean pair $\{e_1,e_2\}$ ($+1$), three
positive vectors $\{e_{+_1},e_{+_2},e_{+_3}\}$ ($+1$), and three negative vectors
$\{e_{-_1},e_{-_2},e_{-_3}\}$ ($-1$). The null working basis is, for $1\le i\le 3$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:null-basis}
  e_{o_i} = e_{+_i}+e_{-_i}, \qquad
  e_{\infty_i} = \tfrac12\,(e_{-_i}-e_{+_i}),
\end{equation}
with inverse $e_{+_i} = \tfrac12 e_{o_i} - e_{\infty_i}$,
$e_{-_i} = \tfrac12 e_{o_i} + e_{\infty_i}$. Each pair $(e_{o_i}, e_{\infty_i})$ is
null, $e_{o_i}^2 = e_{\infty_i}^2 = 0$, with hyperbolic pairing
$e_{o_i}\!\cdot e_{\infty_i} = -1$, and the three pairs are mutually orthogonal.
The resulting Gram matrix is
\begin{equation}
  \begin{tabular}{l|cccccccc}
    & $e_1$ & $e_2$ & $e_{o_1}$ & $e_{\infty_1}$ & $e_{o_2}$ & $e_{\infty_2}$ & $e_{o_3}$ & $e_{\infty_3}$ \\
    \hline
    $e_1$          & 1 & . & . & . & . & . & . & . \\
    $e_2$          & . & 1 & . & . & . & . & . & . \\
    $e_{o_1}$      & . & . & 0 & -1& . & . & . & . \\
    $e_{\infty_1}$ & . & . & -1& 0 & . & . & . & . \\
    $e_{o_2}$      & . & . & . & . & 0 & -1& . & . \\
    $e_{\infty_2}$ & . & . & . & . & -1& 0 & . & . \\
    $e_{o_3}$      & . & . & . & . & . & . & 0 & -1\\
    $e_{\infty_3}$ & . & . & . & . & . & . & -1& 0
  \end{tabular}
  \label{eq:gram}
\end{equation}
This Gram matrix follows directly from the null-basis
definitions~\eqref{eq:null-basis}.

\begin{remark}
% Folded from the 2019 QC2GA draft, which used the symmetric variant.
A symmetric normalisation $e_{o_i}=\tfrac{1}{\sqrt2}(e_{-_i}-e_{+_i})$,
$e_{\infty_i}=\tfrac{1}{\sqrt2}(e_{+_i}+e_{-_i})$ yields the same metric of
\cref{eq:gram} and is convenient for deriving rotation/translation/scaling
versors. We adopt the asymmetric convention~\eqref{eq:null-basis} throughout, as
it makes the point embedding~\eqref{eq:point} carry the conic coefficients
directly; the two differ only by a fixed rescaling of each null pair.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Special blades and the pseudoscalar}
The following blades structure every later construction:
\begin{subequations}\label{eq:special-blades}
\begin{align}
  \eo &= e_{o_1}+e_{o_2}, & \einf &= \tfrac12(e_{\infty_1}+e_{\infty_2}),\\
  \eobar &= e_{o_1}-e_{o_2}, & \einfbar &= \tfrac12(e_{\infty_1}-e_{\infty_2}),\\
  \Iod &= \eobar \joinp e_{o_3}, & \Iinfd &= (e_{\infty_1}-e_{\infty_2})\joinp e_{\infty_3},\\
  \Io &= e_{o_1}\joinp e_{o_2}\joinp e_{o_3}, & \Iinf &= e_{\infty_1}\joinp e_{\infty_2}\joinp e_{\infty_3},\\
  I_\epsilon &= e_1\joinp e_2, & I &= I_\epsilon \joinp \Iinf \joinp \Io .
\end{align}
\end{subequations}
\Cref{tab:blades} summarises these blades, their grades, and their geometric role.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:blade-relations}
The grade-$1$ special blades satisfy
\begin{equation}\label{eq:blade-ip}
  \eo\!\cdot\einf = -1, \qquad
  \eobar\!\cdot\einfbar = -1, \qquad
  e_{o_3}\!\cdot e_{\infty_3} = -1,
\end{equation}
and the two pairs are mutually orthogonal,
$\{\eo,\einf\}\perp\{\eobar,\einfbar\}$. The pseudoscalar squares to
$I^2=-1$, hence $I^{-1}=-I$ and $I\,I^{-1}=1$.
\end{proposition}
\noindent These relations follow by direct computation from \cref{eq:gram}.

\subsection{Geometric meaning of the special blades}
\label{sec:blade-meaning}
The eight null directions group into the three conjugate pairs of \cref{eq:gram},
each carrying a distinct piece of conic structure. On the \emph{origin} side,
$\eo = e_{o_1}+e_{o_2}$ is the combined origin, $\eobar = e_{o_1}-e_{o_2}$ the
\emph{differential} origin, and $e_{o_3}$ the \emph{cross-term} origin; the
\emph{infinity} side mirrors them with the isotropic $\einf$, the differential
$\einfbar$, and the cross-term $e_{\infty_3}$. The three pairs govern,
respectively, the \emph{size}/isotropy of a round object (the radius, carried by
$\einf$), the $x^2-y^2$ \emph{anisotropy} that distinguishes the two axes (carried
by $\eobar,\einfbar$), and the $xy$ \emph{tilt} (carried by $e_{o_3},e_{\infty_3}$).

This split is the algebraic form of the principle that \emph{shape lives on the
origin side and size on the infinity side}: in the dual conic
vector~\eqref{eq:ipns-vector}, the quadratic coefficients $A,B,C$ sit on
$e_{o_1},e_{o_2},e_{o_3}$ while the constant $F$ sits on $e_{\infty_1}+e_{\infty_2}$.
The infinity-gauge blade $\Iinfd=(e_{\infty_1}-e_{\infty_2})\joinp e_{\infty_3}$ is
the pair of \emph{circular points} $I,J$ at infinity through which every circle
passes; it controls the round sub-family and the intersection reductions of
\cref{sec:intersections}.

\subsection{Subspace structure}
\label{sec:subspace}
The eight-dimensional space splits as $\mathbb{R}^8 = V_6 \oplus W_2$, where
\begin{equation}\label{eq:V6-W2}
  V_6 = \mathrm{span}\{\eo,e_1,e_2,e_{\infty_1},e_{\infty_2},e_{\infty_3}\},
  \qquad
  W_2 = \mathrm{span}\{\eobar, e_{o_3}\}.
\end{equation}
$V_6$ is the \emph{point/conic space} --- it carries the six Veronese coordinates
$(1,x,y,x^2,y^2,xy)$ of a conic --- while $W_2$ is the two-dimensional
\emph{gauge plane}, whose top blade is exactly $\Iod = \eobar\joinp e_{o_3}$. This
is why $\Iod$ is \emph{the} gauge blade used to fix the outer-product conic to
grade $7$ (\cref{sec:conics}): it supplies precisely the two directions that the
points cannot reach. The proof that the point
embedding~\eqref{eq:point} lands in $V_6$ (and never in $W_2$) is given in
\cref{sec:point}.

\subsection{Reciprocity identities}
\label{sec:reciprocity}
The two gauge blades $\Iod$ and $\Iinfd$ are reciprocal, and the line at infinity
factors through $\Iinfd$:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:reciprocity}
  \Iinfd \joinp \einf = -\,\Iinf,
  \qquad
  e_{\infty_3}\joinp\einfbar = -\tfrac12\,\Iinfd,
  \qquad
  \Iinfd \,\lrcorner\, \Iod = -2 .
\end{equation}
The last identity makes the contraction $\,\lrcorner\,\Iinfd$ the inverse of the
wedge $\joinp\,\Iod$, which is the algebraic mechanism behind the conic-intersection
pipeline of \cref{sec:intersections}; the first lets a line, as a degenerate conic,
carry the whole line at infinity $\Iinf$.

\subsection{Relation to GAC}
\label{sec:gac}
CCGA and the Geometric Algebra for Conics (GAC)~\cite{hrdina_geometric_2018} are
the same algebra in different bases. Writing the GAC basis
$\left(e_1,e_2,\bar n_+, n_+,\bar n_-, n_-,\bar n_\times, n_\times\right)$ in terms
of the CCGA null basis,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gac-dictionary}
  \bar n_+ = \eo,\quad n_+ = \einf,\quad
  \bar n_- = \eobar,\quad n_- = \einfbar,\quad
  \bar n_\times = e_{o_3},\quad n_\times = e_{\infty_3},
\end{equation}
exhibits the isomorphism as a pure change of basis; the full equivalence is
established in~\cite{chomicki_intersection_2023}. The GAC names match the geometric
roles of \cref{sec:blade-meaning}: the $+$ pair is isotropic (size), the $-$ pair
is the $x^2-y^2$ anisotropy, and the $\times$ pair is the $xy$ cross term.

\section{The Point Embedding}
\label{sec:point}
The conic point map $\mathbb{R}^2 \to \mathbb{R}^8$ is the Veronese-type embedding
\begin{equation}\label{eq:point}
  \mathrm{p} = \eo + \mathrm{p}_\epsilon
     + \tfrac12\,(x^2\,e_{\infty_1} + y^2\,e_{\infty_2}) + xy\,e_{\infty_3},
  \qquad \mathrm{p}_\epsilon = x\,e_1 + y\,e_2 .
\end{equation}
Note that the null vector $e_{o_3}$ does not appear in a point: this preserves
the C2GA property that the inner product of two points is (minus half) their
squared Euclidean distance. For $\mathrm{p}_1,\mathrm{p}_2$ at $(x_1,y_1)$,
$(x_2,y_2)$,
% Folded from the 2019 QC2GA draft (point distance derivation).
\begin{align}\label{eq:point-distance}
  \mathrm{p}_1\!\cdot \mathrm{p}_2
  &= -\tfrac{x_1^2}{2}-\tfrac{x_2^2}{2}-\tfrac{y_1^2}{2}-\tfrac{y_2^2}{2}
     + x_1x_2 + y_1y_2 \notag\\
  &= -\tfrac12\big((x_1-x_2)^2+(y_1-y_2)^2\big)
   = -\tfrac12\,\mathrm{Dist}^2(\mathrm{p}_1,\mathrm{p}_2),
\end{align}
so in particular $\mathrm{p}^2 = 0$ (every point is null). Finite points are
normalised by $\mathrm{p}\cdot\einf=-1$; rescaling a round object by
$-(\,\cdot\,)\cdot\einf$ restores this canonical scale.

\paragraph{Points at infinity.}
Sending $\mathrm{p}(t\mathbf{v})$ to infinity along a direction
$\mathbf{v}=(v_x,v_y)$ and keeping the leading (degree-$2$) term defines the
\emph{ideal point}
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ideal-point}
  v_\infty(\mathbf{v}) \;=\; \lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{\mathrm{p}(t\mathbf{v})}{t^2}
  \;=\; \tfrac{v_x^2}{2}\,e_{\infty_1} + \tfrac{v_y^2}{2}\,e_{\infty_2}
        + v_xv_y\,e_{\infty_3},
\end{equation}
a null vector in $\Iinf$, defined up to scale and independent of the sign of
$\mathbf{v}$ (directions at infinity are unoriented). The ideal points sweep
the Veronese conic inside the plane at infinity; the two \emph{circular
points} are the complex pair $I=v_\infty(1,i)$, $J=v_\infty(1,-i)$, whose real
bivector span is the infinity gauge,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:circular-points}
  I\joinp J = -i\,\Iinfd ,
\end{equation}
so that ``containing the factor $\Iinfd$'' and ``passing through the circular
points'' are the same condition --- the classical characterisation of
circles~\cite{richter-gebert_perspectives_2011}. We write
$v_\infty'(\mathbf{v})$ for the tangent direction to the Veronese conic at
$v_\infty(\mathbf{v})$; the pair $v_\infty\joinp v_\infty'$ encodes a
\emph{double} (tangential) contact with the line at infinity and is the
infinity gauge of the parabola in \cref{sec:axis-aligned}.

\section{Conics: OPNS and IPNS}
\label{sec:conics}
\subsection{The grade-1 IPNS conic}
The fundamental fact of CCGA is that a single grade-$1$ vector cuts out a
general conic by its inner-product null space. For an arbitrary vector
$s = s_{o_1}e_{o_1}+s_{o_2}e_{o_2}+s_{o_3}e_{o_3}+s_{e_1}e_1+s_{e_2}e_2
+ s_{\infty_1}e_{\infty_1}+s_{\infty_2}e_{\infty_2}+s_{\infty_3}e_{\infty_3}$
and a point $\mathrm{p}=\mathrm{p}(x,y)$ of \cref{eq:point}, expanding
$\mathrm{p}\cdot s$ with the Gram matrix~\eqref{eq:gram} gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ipns-conic}
  \mathrm{p}\cdot s = 0 \iff A x^2 + B y^2 + C xy + D x + E y + F = 0 ,
\end{equation}
with the coefficient dictionary
\begin{equation}\label{eq:coeff-map}
  A=-\tfrac{s_{o_1}}{2},\;\; B=-\tfrac{s_{o_2}}{2},\;\; C=-s_{o_3},\;\;
  D=s_{e_1},\;\; E=s_{e_2},\;\; F=-(s_{\infty_1}{+}s_{\infty_2}).
\end{equation}
Two directions are \emph{gauge-inert}: $\mathrm{p}\cdot s$ never depends on
$s_{\infty_3}$, and depends on $s_{\infty_1},s_{\infty_2}$ only through their
sum --- i.e.\ adding any multiple of $e_{\infty_3}$ or of $\einfbar$ to $s$
does not move the locus. The canonical (gauge-fixed) dual conic therefore
lives in the six-dimensional space
$\mathrm{span}\{e_1,e_2,e_{o_1},e_{o_2},e_{o_3},\einf\}$, matching the six
projective coefficients $(A:B:C:D:E:F)$, and exhibits the principle of
\cref{sec:blade-meaning}: shape $(A,B,C)$ on the origin side, size $F$ on the
infinity side.

\subsection{The grade-7 OPNS conic and the $\Iod$ gauge}
\begin{equation}\label{eq:opns-conic}
  \mathbf{c} = \Iod \joinp \mathrm{p}_1 \joinp \cdots \joinp \mathrm{p}_5,
  \qquad \mathbf{c}^{*} = \mathbf{c}\,I^{-1}\ \text{is the grade-1 conic vector}.
\end{equation}
% Folded from the 2019 QC2GA draft (explicit blade-form dual derivation).
Expanding the five-point wedge and collecting the seven blade components --- two
of which share a coefficient --- gives the closed blade form of $\mathbf{c}$
whose dual is the grade-$1$ conic vector $\mathbf{c}^{*}$ of
\cref{eq:ipns-conic}:
\begin{align}\label{eq:opns-blade-form}
  \mathbf{c}
  &= 2A\,(I_\epsilon \joinp e_{\infty_2}\joinp e_{\infty_3}\joinp \Io)
   + 2B\,(I_\epsilon \joinp e_{\infty_1}\joinp e_{\infty_3}\joinp \Io)
   + C\,(I_\epsilon \joinp e_{\infty_1}\joinp e_{\infty_2}\joinp \Io) \notag\\
  &\quad
   - D\,(e_2 \joinp \Iinf \joinp \Io)
   - E\,(e_1 \joinp \Iinf \joinp \Io)
   - \tfrac12 F\,(I_\epsilon \joinp \Iinf \joinp \eo)
   \;=\; \mathbf{c}^{*}\,I,\\
  \mathbf{c}^{*}
  &= -\big(2A\,e_{o_1} + 2B\,e_{o_2} + C\,e_{o_3}\big)
     + D\,e_1 + E\,e_2 - F\,\einf .
  \label{eq:ipns-vector}
\end{align}
The reading $\mathbf{p}\cdot\mathbf{c}^{*}=0 \iff \mathbf{p}\wedge\mathbf{c}=0$
recovers the locus~\eqref{eq:ipns-conic}, so the six conic coefficients sit
directly in the grade-$1$ vector~\eqref{eq:ipns-vector}.

\subsection{Axis-aligned conics by infinity gauge}
\label{sec:axis-aligned}
% Folded from the 2019 QC2GA draft (axis-aligned conic from 4 points).
An axis-aligned conic $A x^2 + B y^2 + D x + E y + F = 0$ has no cross term, i.e.
$C=0$, leaving five coefficients and four degrees of freedom; it can therefore be
built from \emph{four} points. The cross term is removed by wedging with
$e_{\infty_3}$, which annihilates the $xy$ carrier of every point:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:point-wedge-einf3}
  \mathrm{p}\joinp e_{\infty_3}
  = \big(\eo + \mathrm{p}_\epsilon
       + \tfrac12(x^2 e_{\infty_1}+y^2 e_{\infty_2})\big)\joinp e_{\infty_3}.
\end{equation}
Hence an axis-aligned conic is a general conic with four points sent to the
$e_{\infty_3}$ direction:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:axis-aligned-conic}
  \mathbf{c} = \Iod \joinp \mathrm{p}_1 \joinp \mathrm{p}_2 \joinp \mathrm{p}_3
                    \joinp \mathrm{p}_4 \joinp e_{\infty_3}.
\end{equation}
Equivalently, $C=0$ is the statement that the $e_{o_3}$ coefficient of
$\mathbf{c}^{*}$ in~\eqref{eq:ipns-vector} vanishes.

\paragraph{The infinity gauge.}
The four points in~\eqref{eq:axis-aligned-conic} carry one redundant degree of
freedom: the \emph{type} of a conic is decided entirely by its two points at
infinity --- its intersection with the line at infinity $\Iinf$. Spending that
degree of freedom, by replacing the fourth point with a fixed grade-$2$
\emph{infinity gauge} blade $B_\infty$, produces an axis-aligned conic of
prescribed type from only three points:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:axis-conic-family}
  \mathbf{c} = \mathrm{p}_1\joinp \mathrm{p}_2\joinp \mathrm{p}_3
               \joinp B_\infty \joinp \Iod,
  \qquad
  B_\infty = (\alpha\,e_{\infty_1} + \beta\,e_{\infty_2})\joinp e_{\infty_3}.
\end{equation}
$B_\infty$ \emph{is} the pair of ideal points of $\mathbf{c}$, the two solutions of
its quadratic part on $\Iinf$; choosing $B_\infty = \mathrm{p}_4\joinp e_{\infty_3}$
recovers the general axis-aligned conic~\eqref{eq:axis-aligned-conic}. The smooth
types are listed in \cref{tab:axis-conics}.

\begin{proposition}[type from the infinity gauge]\label{prop:axis-type}
The conic~\eqref{eq:axis-conic-family} has $C=0$ and quadratic coefficients
$A\propto\alpha$, $B\propto-\beta$, hence discriminant
$\Delta_2 = AB \propto -\alpha\beta$. Therefore
\begin{gather}
  \alpha\beta<0 \Rightarrow \Delta_2>0\ (\text{ellipse}),\qquad
  \alpha\beta=0 \Rightarrow \Delta_2=0\ (\text{parabola}),\notag\\
  \alpha\beta>0 \Rightarrow \Delta_2<0\ (\text{hyperbola}),
\end{gather}
which is the projective classification by incidence with $\Iinf$: an imaginary,
double, or real ideal pair (the discriminants $\Delta_2,\Delta_3$ are defined in
\cref{sec:extraction}).
\end{proposition}

The ideal pairs are wedges of the at-infinity points of \cref{sec:point}: the real
pair $v_\infty(a,b)\joinp v_\infty(a,-b)\equiv(a^2 e_{\infty_1}+b^2 e_{\infty_2})\joinp e_{\infty_3}$
(hyperbola), its imaginary counterpart $(a^2 e_{\infty_1}-b^2 e_{\infty_2})\joinp e_{\infty_3}$
(ellipse), and the double contact $v_\infty(1,0)\joinp v_\infty'(1,0)\equiv e_{\infty_1}\joinp e_{\infty_3}$
(parabola). The circle is the isotropic case $\alpha=-\beta$, where $B_\infty=\Iinfd$
is the circular-point pair of \cref{sec:blade-meaning}; the construction then
specialises to the circle, and \eqref{eq:axis-conic-family} to the standard
axis-aligned ellipse, up to scale.

\paragraph{Degenerate conics.}
Within each $\Delta_2$ class a degenerate member ($\Delta_3=0$) appears when the
three points are placed in special position; $B_\infty$ still fixes $\Delta_2$,
while $\Delta_3$ drops to zero:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \emph{Two crossing lines} (degenerate hyperbola): $B_\infty=(e_{\infty_1}+e_{\infty_2})\joinp e_{\infty_3}$
    with the three points through the common centre, e.g.\ yielding $x^2-y^2=0$.
  \item \emph{Two parallel lines} (degenerate parabola): $B_\infty=e_{\infty_2}\joinp e_{\infty_3}$
    with three points on two vertical lines, e.g.\ $x^2-1=0$.
  \item \emph{A point} (degenerate ellipse, two imaginary lines): the $r\to0$ limit
    of an imaginary circle, $x^2+y^2=0$; having no three distinct real points, it
    arises only as a limit.
\end{itemize}
Since the type test keys on $\Delta_2$ alone, a degenerate hyperbola still reports
as a hyperbola; genuineness is the separate condition $\Delta_3\neq0$. This places
the whole family on the classical six-cell classification of
\cref{tab:axis-master}: $B_\infty$ chooses the column ($\Delta_2$) and the point
configuration chooses the row ($\Delta_3$).

quadric-surface variants are out of scope for this two-dimensional foundation and
belong to the QCGA lift discussed in the conclusion.

\subsection{Axis-aligned conics by dual construction}
\label{sec:axis-aligned-dual}
Dually, an axis-aligned conic is the grade-$1$ vector $s=\mathbf{c}^{*}$
of~\eqref{eq:ipns-vector} with $C=0$. Splitting the origin part with $e_o=e_{o_1}+e_{o_2}$
and $\eobar=e_{o_1}-e_{o_2}$, so that $-2A\,e_{o_1}-2B\,e_{o_2}=-(A{+}B)\,e_o-(A{-}B)\,\eobar$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:dual-axis-conic}
  s = -(A{+}B)\,e_o \;-\;(A{-}B)\,\eobar \;+\; D\,e_1 + E\,e_2 \;-\; F\,\einf .
\end{equation}
A point lies on the conic iff $\mathrm{q}\cdot s = 0$~\eqref{eq:ipns-conic}. For the
centred conics ($D=E=0$, $F=-1$) this gives the closed forms of \cref{tab:dual-axis-conics};
the circle is the familiar centre-point form $\mathrm{p}_c-\tfrac{r^2}{2}\einf$.

\paragraph{The anisotropic round point.}
The circle's $\mathrm{p}_c-\tfrac12 r^2\einf$ generalises by giving the point two
independent weights, one per conformal origin. For weights $w_1,w_2$ define the \emph{anisotropic point} at $(c_x,c_y)$ as one
independently weighted bundle per axis,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:aniso-point}
  \mathrm{p}^{(w_1,w_2)} =
     w_1\big(e_{o_1}+c_x e_1+\tfrac{c_x^2}{2}e_{\infty_1}\big)
   + w_2\big(e_{o_2}+c_y e_2+\tfrac{c_y^2}{2}e_{\infty_2}\big)
   + w_1 w_2\, c_x c_y\,e_{\infty_3},
\end{equation}
which is \emph{null for every} $w_1,w_2$ --- the per-axis pairings
$e_{o_i}\!\cdot e_{\infty_i}=-1$ cancel axis by axis --- and obeys, for a test
point $\mathrm{q}=\mathrm{q}(x,y)$, the weighted distance identity
$\mathrm{q}\cdot\mathrm{p}^{(w_1,w_2)} = -\tfrac12\big[w_1(x-c_x)^2 + w_2(y-c_y)^2\big]$.
Attaching a radius yields a \emph{single dual vector for every axis-aligned conic},
the \emph{anisotropic round point}
\begin{multline}\label{eq:aniso-round-point}
  \sigma(c_x,c_y,r,w_1,w_2) =
     w_1\big(e_{o_1}+c_x e_1+\tfrac{c_x^2}{2}e_{\infty_1}\big)
   + w_2\big(e_{o_2}+c_y e_2+\tfrac{c_y^2}{2}e_{\infty_2}\big)\\
   + w_1 w_2\, c_x c_y\,e_{\infty_3}
   - \tfrac12 r^2\,\einf,
\end{multline}
whose locus $\mathrm{q}\cdot\sigma=0$ is the conic
$w_1(x-c_x)^2 + w_2(y-c_y)^2 = r^2$, with quadratic coefficients $A=w_1$, $B=w_2$
and square $\sigma^2 = \tfrac12 r^2(w_1+w_2)$. The \textbf{weight ratio
$w_1:w_2 = 1/a^2 : 1/b^2$ fixes the $(a,b)$ shape} and $r$ fixes the size, so
\eqref{eq:aniso-round-point} realises \emph{every} axis-aligned conic: $w_1=w_2$
gives a circle, $w_1,w_2>0$ an ellipse (semi-axes $a,b$ for $w_i=1/a_i^2$, $r=1$),
and $w_2<0$ a hyperbola. The sign of the radius term is the reality
($-\tfrac12 r^2\einf$ the real conic, $+\tfrac12 r^2\einf$ its imaginary partner),
exactly as for the circle, whose ordinary round point $\mathrm{p}_c-\tfrac12 r^2\einf$
is the isotropic case $w_1=w_2=1$. Expression~\eqref{eq:aniso-round-point} agrees
with the canonical vector of \cref{tab:dual-axis-conics} up to the gauge-inert
$\einfbar,e_{\infty_3}$ terms, which do not move the locus.

\begin{remark}[why CCGA, and why this is still a conic]\label{rem:two-origins}
CGA's round point $\mathrm{p}_c-\tfrac12 r^2\einf$ has a single weight and can
carry only an isotropic radius (circles); CCGA's \emph{two} conformal origins
$e_{o_1},e_{o_2}$ let the round point carry an \emph{anisotropic} radius, i.e. an
ellipse or hyperbola. The weights are exactly the conic's quadratic coefficients
$(A,B)=(w_1,w_2)$, and allowing the off-diagonal weight on $e_{o_3}$ adds the cross
term $C$, recovering the general (tilted) dual conic. A ``fully weighted point''
is therefore a quadratic form --- a conic --- rather than a new primitive: the
isotropic point of \cref{sec:point} remains the base element of the algebra, and
$\sigma$ is read as a \emph{derived} round object.
\end{remark}

\begin{proposition}[type from the origin balance]\label{prop:dual-axis-type}
In~\eqref{eq:dual-axis-conic} the conic type is set by the balance of the
combined and differential origins, $\eobar/e_o$: writing $A=1/a^2$ and
$B=\pm1/b^2$,
\begin{gather}
  |A{-}B|<|A{+}B|\ (\text{ellipse}),\qquad
  |A{-}B|=|A{+}B|\ (\text{parabola}),\notag\\
  |A{-}B|>|A{+}B|\ (\text{hyperbola}),
\end{gather}
the circle being the isotropic limit $\eobar=0$ ($A=B$), and the parabola the
case where one of $A,B$ vanishes.
\end{proposition}

This is the dual of Proposition~\ref{prop:axis-type}: the wedge construction fixes the type by
the \emph{infinity} gauge $B_\infty$ (the conic's ideal points), whereas the dual
construction fixes it by the \emph{origin} blades $e_o,\eobar$ (its shape) --- the
gauge duality ``shape on the origin side, size on the infinity side'' of
\cref{sec:blade-meaning}. The circle's clean centre-point form
$\mathrm{p}_c-\tfrac{r^2}{2}\einf$ is exactly the isotropic case where the
$\eobar$ term drops out, while the ``$\pm$'' that distinguishes ellipse from
hyperbola is the sign carried by the $\eobar$ anisotropy term. Off-centre conics
follow by a translator versor (\cref{sec:transforms}), and the degenerate members
($\Delta_3=0$) are the same special-position limits as on the wedge side.

\section{The Object Taxonomy}
\label{sec:taxonomy}
\subsection{The construction ladder}

Every CCGA object is a wedge of points with at most the two gauge blades $\Iod$
(origin) and $\Iinfd$ (infinity, the circular-point pair). Three ladders organise
the whole zoo, distinguished by which gauge they carry.

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Bare multipole ladder} (no gauge): the plain wedge of $k$ points,
  $\mathrm{p}_1\joinp\cdots\joinp\mathrm{p}_k$, grade $k$ for $k=1,\dots,5$
  (point, twopole, tripole, quadpole, pentapole). Five points already fix a conic
  as a point-locus; the pentapole is the last rung.
\item \textbf{$\Iod$-gauged conic ladder}: wedging $\Iod$ promotes a blade by two
  grades and gauge-fixes it so the dual is clean (\cref{sec:conics}). For $n\le 4$
  points $\mathrm{p}_1\joinp\cdots\joinp\mathrm{p}_n\joinp\Iod$ (grade $n+2$) is a
  \emph{pencil} --- the gauge-fixed family of conics through the $n$ points; the
  $n=2$ rung is the \emph{gauged dipole} (the conic-gauge point pair, the natural
  conic\,$\meet$\,line result) and the $n=4$ rung is the conic\,$\meet$\,conic
  intersection blade. \emph{A full CCGA conic is precisely a grade-$7$ blade ending
  in $\joinp\,\Iod$.} The general conic is
  $\mathrm{p}_1\joinp\cdots\joinp\mathrm{p}_5\joinp\Iod$; the smooth named conics are
  \emph{three points $+$ an ideal-point pair $+\,\Iod$}, the pair being two real
  ideal points (hyperbola, $\Delta_2>0$), one double ideal point (parabola, tangent
  to $\Iinf$, $\Delta_2=0$), or two imaginary ideal points (ellipse, $\Delta_2<0$).
  When that pair is the circular points $\Iinfd$ the ellipse becomes a circle ---
  which is therefore a CGA object (next ladder), not listed here.
\item \textbf{CGA embedded in CCGA} (the $\Iinfd$ sub-array): wedging the
  circular-point pair $\Iinfd$ embeds the standard CGA round/flat family inside CCGA
  at grade $=\text{(CGA grade)}+2$. Round point $\mathrm{p}\joinp\Iinfd$, point pair
  $\mathrm{p}_1\joinp\mathrm{p}_2\joinp\Iinfd$, circle
  $\mathrm{p}_1\joinp\mathrm{p}_2\joinp\mathrm{p}_3\joinp\Iinfd$, with the flat
  versions (flat point, line) carrying an extra $\einf$. These are CGA objects, not
  full CCGA conics.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Reality conditions}
\label{sec:reality}
Wedges of real points always produce real objects, but the constructions of
\cref{tab:zoo} extend by linearity to multivectors with no real locus
(imaginary circles, conjugate point pairs), and the algebra detects this by a
sign --- always read from a \emph{square}, never from a raw coefficient, so
that the test is invariant under rescaling.

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Points} are null, $\mathrm{p}^2=0$~\eqref{eq:point-distance}.
  A \emph{round point} (point with radius) $\mathrm{p}_c\mp\tfrac{r^2}{2}\einf$
  squares to $\pm r^2$: positive for a real circle, negative for its imaginary
  partner. The scale-invariant radius of any round grade-$1$ object is
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:invariant-radius}
    r^2 = \frac{s^2}{(s\cdot\einf)^2},
  \end{equation}
  so $\mathrm{sign}\,s^2$ separates \emph{real} ($>0$), \emph{degenerate /
  tangent} ($=0$), and \emph{imaginary} ($<0$). Ideal points have
  $s\cdot\einf=0$: they carry no radius, and reality at infinity is read from
  the sign of the quadratic form (\cref{sec:extraction}).
\item \textbf{Twopoles} $\mathrm{P}=\mathrm{p}_1\joinp\mathrm{p}_2$ carry their
  reality in $\mathrm{P}^2$: positive for two real points, zero for a
  coincident (tangent) pair, negative for a complex-conjugate pair. The same
  test governs the CGA point pair $\mathrm{p}_1\joinp\mathrm{p}_2\joinp\Iinfd$
  and the dipoles extracted in \cref{sec:intersections}.
\item \textbf{Conics} use the pair of discriminants
  $(\Delta_2,\Delta_3)$ of \cref{sec:extraction}: $\Delta_3\neq0$ selects the
  smooth types (ellipse/parabola/hyperbola by the sign of $\Delta_2$, as in
  \cref{tab:axis-master}), $\Delta_3=0$ the degenerate ones; within the
  ellipse class, real versus imaginary is the sign of $s^2$ as for rounds.
\end{itemize}

\section{Operations: Join, Meet, and Dual}
\label{sec:operations}
Because the metric of $\mathbb{R}_{5,3}$ is non-degenerate, the pseudoscalar is
invertible (Proposition~\ref{prop:blade-relations}) and the three incidence operations
are defined for every pair of blades, with one orientation convention fixed
once and used everywhere:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:operations}
  A^{*} = A\,I^{-1}, \qquad
  (A^{*})\,I = A, \qquad
  A \meet B = \big(A^{*}\joinp B^{*}\big)^{*}.
\end{equation}
The \emph{join} $\joinp$ spans: it is the construction ladder of
\cref{sec:taxonomy}, and grades add when the factors share no common subspace.
The \emph{dual} exchanges the two null-space representations of the same
object,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:incidence}
  \mathrm{q}\joinp \mathbf{c} = 0
  \;\iff\;
  \mathrm{q}\cdot \mathbf{c}^{*} = 0 ,
\end{equation}
the outer-product (OPNS) and inner-product (IPNS) incidence tests of
\cref{eq:opns-conic,eq:ipns-conic}. The \emph{meet} $\meet$ intersects.

\paragraph{Grade arithmetic.}
For blades in general position the meet obeys
\begin{equation}\label{eq:meet-grade}
  \operatorname{gr}(A \meet B) = \operatorname{gr}(A) + \operatorname{gr}(B) - 8 ,
\end{equation}
so a meet is geometrically non-trivial only when the grades sum to at least
$8$. At exactly $8$ the meet collapses to a scalar, which is precisely the
incidence test: for a conic $\mathbf{c}$ (grade $7$) and a point $\mathrm{q}$
(grade $1$), $\mathrm{q}\meet\mathbf{c}=0$ iff $\mathrm{q}$ lies on
$\mathbf{c}$, in agreement with~\eqref{eq:incidence}. Below the threshold the
regressive product vanishes identically: two low-grade point-objects do not
\emph{meet} --- they \emph{join}. This asymmetry (join builds, meet
intersects, meet needs grade-sum $\ge 8$) organises the whole interaction
calculus of \cref{sec:intersections}, where the meaningful meets of the
grade-$7$ conic are tabulated.

\paragraph{Gauge stripping.}
The conic constructions of \cref{sec:taxonomy} end in $\joinp\,\Iod$; their
meets therefore carry an $\Iod$ factor that must be removed before the point
content can be read. The reciprocity identity $\Iinfd\,\lrcorner\,\Iod = -2$
of \cref{eq:reciprocity} makes the contraction $X\mapsto\Iinfd\lrcorner X$
invert the wedge $X\mapsto X\joinp\Iod$ on such blades: wedging with the
origin gauge is undone by contracting with the infinity gauge. This single
mechanism powers the quadpole recovery of \cref{sec:intersections} and the
centre extraction of \cref{sec:extraction}.

\section{Geometric-Algebra-Native Feature Extraction}
\label{sec:extraction}
Every classical feature of a conic --- type, centre, axes, semi-axes,
eccentricity, foci, asymptotic directions, tangent and polar lines --- can be
read directly off the dual vector $s=\mathbf{c}^{*}$ by inner products with
the special blades of \cref{tab:blades} and meets, with no coordinate matrix,
no eigensolver, and no linear system. The only steps that leave the algebra
are $\sqrt{\ }$ and $\operatorname{atan2}$, the irreducible scalar content of
axis lengths and angles.

\subsection{Coefficients and the trace--deviatoric split}
Pairing the canonical dual vector~\eqref{eq:ipns-vector} with the Gram
matrix~\eqref{eq:gram} inverts the coefficient dictionary~\eqref{eq:coeff-map}:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:coeff-extract}
  A=\tfrac12\, s\!\cdot e_{\infty_1},\quad
  B=\tfrac12\, s\!\cdot e_{\infty_2},\quad
  C= s\!\cdot e_{\infty_3},\quad
  D= s\!\cdot e_1,\quad
  E= s\!\cdot e_2,\quad
  F= s\!\cdot \eo .
\end{equation}
The quadratic part splits along the conjugate pairs of
\cref{sec:blade-meaning} into an \emph{isotropic} scalar and a
\emph{deviatoric} spin-$2$ pair:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:trace-deviatoric}
  \underbrace{A+B = s\!\cdot\einf}_{\text{trace (size)}},
  \qquad
  \underbrace{(A-B,\;C) = (s\!\cdot\einfbar,\; s\!\cdot e_{\infty_3})}_{\text{anisotropy; polar angle } = 2\theta_{\text{axis}}} .
\end{equation}
The deviatoric pair is a symmetric-square (Veronese) object: its polar angle
is \emph{twice} the axis angle of the conic, which is also why the rotation
versor of \cref{sec:transforms} acts on it with angle $2\alpha$.

\subsection{Type: the discriminants as inner products and meets}
\label{sec:discriminants}
The shape discriminant $\Delta_2$ is a closed quadratic form in the three
infinity pairings of~\eqref{eq:trace-deviatoric}:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:delta2}
  \Delta_2 \;=\; AB-\tfrac{C^2}{4}
  \;=\; \tfrac14\Big[(s\!\cdot\einf)^2-(s\!\cdot\einfbar)^2-(s\!\cdot e_{\infty_3})^2\Big].
\end{equation}
The determinant discriminant $\Delta_3=\det M_3$ (with $M_3$ the symmetric
coefficient matrix of the conic) is obtained as a \emph{meet of three dual
lines}~\cite{chomicki_intersection_2023}. The three rows of $M_3$ are the
polar lines (cf.~\cref{sec:polars}) of the two ideal basis directions and of
the origin; as grade-$7$ blades,
\begin{gather}
  \ell_1=\big(A e_1+\tfrac C2 e_2-\tfrac D2 \einf\big) I,\qquad
  \ell_2=\big(\tfrac C2 e_1+B e_2-\tfrac E2 \einf\big) I,\notag\\
  \ell_3=\big(\tfrac D2 e_1+\tfrac E2 e_2-F \einf\big) I,
  \label{eq:three-lines}
\end{gather}
and their meets evaluate, in closed form,
\begin{align}
  \ell_1\meet\ell_2
  &= \tfrac12\big(\Delta_2\,\eo + x_c\,e_1 + y_c\,e_2\big)\joinp\Iod\joinp\Iinf,
  \label{eq:center-meet}\\
  \ell_1\meet\ell_2\meet\ell_3
  &= \tfrac12\,\Delta_3\;\Iod\joinp\Iinf,
  \label{eq:delta3-meet}
\end{align}
with $x_c = \tfrac{CE}4-\tfrac{BD}2$ and $y_c=\tfrac{CD}4-\tfrac{AE}2$. The
pair $(\Delta_2,\Delta_3)$ then classifies the conic by
\cref{tab:axis-master}: the sign of $\Delta_2$ chooses
ellipse/parabola/hyperbola, and $\Delta_3=0$ flags the degenerate members.

\subsection{Centre}
\label{sec:center}
Equation~\eqref{eq:center-meet} exhibits the centre as a geometric object: the
grade-$6$ blade $\ell_1\meet\ell_2$ is, up to the gauge factor
$\Iod\joinp\Iinf$, the \emph{flat point}
$\mathrm{p}_c = \Delta_2\,\eo + x_c\,e_1+y_c\,e_2$ in homogeneous form ---
the pole of the line at infinity. For a central conic ($\Delta_2\neq0$) the
affine centre is $(x_c/\Delta_2,\,y_c/\Delta_2)$; for a parabola
($\Delta_2=0$) the $\eo$ component vanishes and the pole of $\Iinf$
degenerates to an \emph{ideal} point in the axis direction --- the parabola's
point of tangency with the line at infinity, which lies on the parabola
itself. Equivalently, in pure inner products: with
$s_i = s\!\cdot e_{\infty_i}$ and $\sigma_k = s\!\cdot e_k$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:center-ip}
  c_x=\frac{s_3\sigma_2-s_2\sigma_1}{s_1 s_2-s_3^2},\qquad
  c_y=\frac{s_3\sigma_1-s_1\sigma_2}{s_1 s_2-s_3^2},\qquad
  s_1 s_2 - s_3^2 = 4\Delta_2 .
\end{equation}

\subsection{Axes, semi-axes, eccentricity, foci}
\label{sec:axes}
The eigen-decomposition of the quadratic form is already solved by the
split~\eqref{eq:trace-deviatoric} --- no eigensolver is needed. The shape
eigenvalues and the axis angle are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:eigen-free}
  \lambda_\pm=\tfrac12\,(s\!\cdot\einf)
  \pm\tfrac12\sqrt{(s\!\cdot\einfbar)^2+(s\!\cdot e_{\infty_3})^2},
  \qquad
  \theta_{\text{axis}}=\tfrac12\operatorname{atan2}\!\big(s\!\cdot e_{\infty_3},\;
  s\!\cdot\einfbar\big),
\end{equation}
and the value of the conic at its own centre is the GA incidence number
$F' = \mathrm{p}_c\!\cdot s$ (with $\mathrm{p}_c$ the embedded centre point),
giving the semi-axes
\begin{equation}\label{eq:semi-axes}
  a_i^2 = -\,\frac{\mathrm{p}_c\!\cdot s}{\lambda_i} .
\end{equation}
Eccentricity and foci follow classically: $c=\sqrt{\,|a^2\mp b^2|\,}$
(ellipse $-$, hyperbola $+$), $e=c/a$, and the foci are
$\mathrm{p}_c \pm c\,\hat u_{\theta_{\text{axis}}}$ (embedded by
\cref{eq:point}); for the parabola the focus sits at focal distance from the
vertex along the axis direction returned by \cref{sec:center}. Note where the
scalar boundary lies: \cref{eq:eigen-free,eq:semi-axes} use exactly one
$\sqrt{\ }$ and one $\operatorname{atan2}$ --- the irreducible content of
lengths and angles --- while everything else is inner products.

\subsection{Asymptotic directions: incidence with infinity}
\label{sec:asymptotes}
An ideal point~\eqref{eq:ideal-point} lies on the conic iff
\begin{equation}\label{eq:asymptote}
  v_\infty(\mathbf{v})\cdot s = 0
  \;\iff\;
  A v_x^2 + C v_x v_y + B v_y^2 = 0 ,
\end{equation}
a binary quadratic whose root structure is the projective type
classification of Proposition~\ref{prop:axis-type}: two real roots give the hyperbola's
asymptotic directions, one double root the parabola's axis direction, two
imaginary roots the ellipse (and the circular points $I,J$ exactly when
$A=B$, $C=0$). As a blade computation this is the meet
$\mathbf{c}\meet\Iinf$ --- grade $7+3-8=2$ by \cref{eq:meet-grade} --- the
conic's \emph{ideal dipole}, whose reality is read from its square as in
\cref{sec:reality}.

\subsection{Tangents and polars}
\label{sec:polars}
The polar line of a point $\mathrm{q}=(x,y)$ is assembled from the point-map
differentials $\partial_x\mathrm{p} = e_1 + x\,e_{\infty_1}+y\,e_{\infty_3}$
and $\partial_y\mathrm{p} = e_2 + y\,e_{\infty_2}+x\,e_{\infty_3}$:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:polar}
  \ell_{\mathrm{q}} :\;
  \underbrace{\tfrac12\big(\partial_x\mathrm{p}\cdot s\big)}_{n_x}\,X
  +\underbrace{\tfrac12\big(\partial_y\mathrm{p}\cdot s\big)}_{n_y}\,Y
  +\underbrace{\big(\eo+\tfrac{x}2 e_1+\tfrac{y}2 e_2\big)\cdot s}_{n_0}
  \;=\;0,
\end{equation}
encoded as the dual line $n_x e_1+n_y e_2-n_0\,\einf$. This is the pole--polar
pairing $M_3(x,y,1)^{\!\top}$ in GA form: when $\mathrm{q}$ lies on the conic
($\mathrm{q}\cdot s=0$) the polar is the \emph{tangent} at $\mathrm{q}$; the
polar of the centre is the line at infinity (consistently with
\cref{sec:center}); and the normal at a contact point is the perpendicular
line through it, $-n_y X + n_x Y + (n_y x - n_x y)=0$. The three dual
lines~\eqref{eq:three-lines} are precisely the polars of the two ideal basis
directions and of the origin, closing the loop with
\cref{sec:discriminants}.

\section{Transformations as Versors}
\label{sec:transforms}
A versor $V$ acts on every object --- point, multipole, conic, pencil blade
--- by the same sandwich product $X \mapsto V X \tilde V$, and the action is
an outermorphism: it distributes over $\joinp$, commutes with the dual, and
therefore preserves incidence~\eqref{eq:incidence}. This section gives the
versors of the planar similarities explicitly, then delimits precisely which
planar maps admit a versor at all.

\subsection{The similarity versors}
Because the point embedding~\eqref{eq:point} is quadratic, each Euclidean
generator acts on \emph{three} carrier pairs at once --- the linear
coordinates on $e_1,e_2$, the squares on $e_{\infty_1},e_{\infty_2}$, and the
cross term on $e_{\infty_3}$ --- and each versor below is the commuting
product of the corresponding simple factors.

\begin{description}
\item[Translation $(t_x,t_y)$] $T = T_x(t_x)\,T_y(t_y)$ with
\begin{align}
  T_x(\tau)&=\Big(1-\tfrac{\tau}2\, e_1\!\joinp e_{\infty_1}\Big)
             \Big(1-\tfrac{\tau}2\, e_2\!\joinp e_{\infty_3}\Big),
  \label{eq:translator}\\
  T_y(\tau)&=\Big(1-\tfrac{\tau}2\, e_2\!\joinp e_{\infty_2}\Big)
             \Big(1-\tfrac{\tau}2\, e_1\!\joinp e_{\infty_3}\Big).\notag
\end{align}
The first factor of each is the familiar CGA translator on the matching
conformal pair; the second updates the cross term $xy$, which a translation
in $x$ changes by $\tau y$.
\item[Rotation by $\alpha$] $R(\alpha) = e^{\alpha E}\, e^{\alpha K}$ with
the commuting pair
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rotor}
  E=-\tfrac12\, e_1\joinp e_2, \qquad
  K=\eobar\joinp e_{\infty_3} - e_{o_3}\joinp\einfbar,
  \qquad K^3 = -4K,
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rotor-exp}
  e^{\alpha E} = \cos\tfrac{\alpha}{2} - \sin\tfrac{\alpha}{2}\, e_1\joinp e_2,
  \qquad
  e^{\alpha K} = 1 + \tfrac{\sin 2\alpha}{2}\,K + \tfrac{1-\cos2\alpha}{4}\,K^2 .
\end{equation}
$E$ rotates the linear carriers at the half angle, as in CGA; $K$ is
\emph{not} simple-exponential at the half angle --- it satisfies $K^3=-4K$
and rotates the deviatoric pair $(\einfbar, e_{\infty_3})$-$(\eobar,e_{o_3})$
at the \emph{double} angle $2\alpha$, the symmetric-square action already met
in \cref{eq:trace-deviatoric}: a conic's anisotropy returns to itself after a
half turn.
\item[Uniform scaling by $s$] With $u=\tfrac12\ln s$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:dilator}
  D(s)=\prod_{i=1}^{3}\big(\cosh u + \sinh u \; e_{o_i}\!\joinp e_{\infty_i}\big),
\end{equation}
one boost per conformal pair, all three commuting.
\item[Reflection] Across the $x$-axis the versor is the grade-$3$ blade
$V_x = e_2\joinp e_{o_3}\joinp e_{\infty_3}$, which negates exactly the
$y$-odd carriers ($e_2$ and the cross-term pair); an arbitrary line of
direction angle $\theta$ through a point $c$ reflects by the conjugate
$T(c)\,R(\theta)\,V_x\,\tilde R(\theta)\,T(-c)$. Likewise rotations and
scalings about an arbitrary centre are the translated conjugates
$T(c)\,V\,T(-c)$.
\end{description}

\subsection{Which planar maps are versors}
\label{sec:versor-group}
The versors above generate, on the full conic family, exactly the
\emph{similarity group} of the plane: translations, rotations, reflections,
and uniform scalings, together with their compositions. The boundary is
sharp on both sides.

\emph{No versor beyond similarities.} A planar map $\varphi$ can act by a
versor only if its lift to the Veronese coordinates
$(1,x,y,\tfrac{x^2}2,\tfrac{y^2}2,xy)$ extends to an \emph{orthogonal} map of
$\mathbb{R}_{5,3}$, since sandwiching preserves the metric. A shear or a
non-uniform scaling does extend to a linear outermorphism of the lift --- it
maps conics to conics projectively --- but the induced linear map fails to
preserve the inner product (it deforms the distance
pairing~\eqref{eq:point-distance}), so no versor exists: such maps act on
CCGA only as outermorphisms, applied matrix-wise, with none of the versor
calculus available. The same holds a fortiori for general projective maps.

\emph{Versors beyond similarities exist, but not on conics.} The round
(CGA) sub-family of \cref{sec:taxonomy} admits the usual conformal
extensions: the invertible grade-$1$ vector
$\sigma = \mathrm{p}_c - \tfrac{r^2}{2}\einf$ ($\sigma^2=r^2$) is a versor
implementing inversion in the circle $(\mathrm{p}_c, r)$, and
$\sigma\,T(\mathbf{b})\,\sigma$ is a transversion. These are M\"obius maps:
they preserve the circle/line family but send a general conic to a
\emph{quartic} curve, which has no CCGA representative. Their sandwich is
still an algebra automorphism --- it maps a grade-$7$ conic blade to a
grade-$7$ blade --- but the resulting conic is \emph{not} the geometric image
of the original curve; the versor represents the M\"obius map on the round
sub-family only. The transformation repertoire of CCGA on \emph{all} conics
is therefore exactly the similarities, with the conformal extension available
on the CGA sub-array --- consistent with GAC, whose additional
conic-preserving versors~\cite{hrdina_geometric_2018} are likewise
similarity-type on the general family.

\section{Conic Intersections}
\label{sec:intersections}
Two conics meet in four points, counted with multiplicity over the complex
projective plane (B\'ezout~\cite{fulton_algebraic_1989}). This section turns
that statement into blade arithmetic: the regressive product delivers every
intersection as a single blade (\cref{sec:meet-arithmetic}), the gauge
mechanism of \cref{sec:operations} strips that blade to a point object
(\cref{sec:pipeline}), and a shared-factor principle predicts, for every pair
in the zoo, how many of the four points are finite and how to land them
(\cref{sec:bezout-reduction}).

\subsection{The universal pipeline}
\label{sec:pipeline}
For two conics $\mathbf{c}_1,\mathbf{c}_2$ in general position the meet is the
grade-$6$ blade
\begin{equation}\label{eq:I6}
  I_6 \;=\; \mathbf{c}_1\meet\mathbf{c}_2
  \;\equiv\; \mathrm{p}_1\joinp\mathrm{p}_2\joinp\mathrm{p}_3\joinp\mathrm{p}_4
             \joinp\Iod
  \;=\; Q\joinp\Iod ,
\end{equation}
the \emph{quadpole} $Q$ of the four intersection points, gauge-fixed by the
same $\Iod$ that turns five points into a grade-$7$ conic --- the
intersection object is itself a rung of the $\Iod$-ladder of
\cref{sec:taxonomy} (the $n=4$ pencil blade). Incidence holds directly on the
blade: $\mathrm{q}\joinp I_6 = 0$ iff $\mathrm{q}$ is one of the four points.
The gauge is stripped by the reciprocity mechanism of \cref{sec:operations},
\begin{equation}\label{eq:quadpole-recovery}
  Q \;\equiv\; \Iinfd \lrcorner\, I_6 ,
\end{equation}
a pure grade-$4$ point object. What remains is to factor the quadpole into
its four points --- and this, by the design principle of \cref{sec:intro}, is
exactly where the algebra hands over to scalar root extraction: splitting
$Q$ amounts to solving a quartic (a degenerate-conic pencil member, a
resolvent cubic, then two $\pm\sqrt{\ }$ dipole splits --- Ferrari's solution
in GA dress, cf.~\cite{faucette_geometric_1996}). The four points come
labelled by the B\'ezout reality split
\begin{equation}\label{eq:reality-split}
  r \;(\text{finite real}) \;+\; m \;(\text{finite conjugate}) \;+\;
  k \;(\text{ideal}) \;=\; 4 ,
\end{equation}
with each dipole's reality read from its square as in \cref{sec:reality}.
This route is valid for \emph{every} pair of conics; the next subsection
shows when it simplifies, and why.

\subsection{Shared-factor / B\'ezout reduction}
\label{sec:bezout-reduction}
Each object of the taxonomy carries an \emph{infinity signature} --- its
intersection with the line at infinity --- fixed once and for all by a blade
in its construction (\cref{tab:infinity-signature}). When two objects share
ideal points by construction, that part of the B\'ezout budget is spent at
infinity:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:shared-factor}
  \#\,\text{finite intersections} \;=\; 4 \;-\;
  \#\,\text{forced common ideal points}.
\end{equation}

\paragraph{The round shortcut.}
When \emph{both} objects contain the circular points --- i.e.\ both belong to
the circle/line (CGA) sub-family --- two of the four B\'ezout points are
exactly $I,J$, the quadpole factors as
$Q \equiv D_2\joinp\Iinfd$ with $D_2$ a clean CGA point pair, and a second
contraction strips the round gauge:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:round-shortcut}
  D_2 \;\equiv\; \Iod\lrcorner\big(\Iinfd\lrcorner\, I_6\big),
  \qquad
  \mathrm{p}_\pm = \big(D_2 \pm \sqrt{D_2^2}\,\big)\,
                   \big(\einf\lrcorner D_2\big)^{-1},
\end{equation}
the classical conformal dipole extraction, with reality given by the sign of
$D_2^2$. We emphasise the validity boundary: the contraction in
\cref{eq:round-shortcut} always \emph{returns} a grade-$2$ object, but it is
the true finite dipole only when both operands pass through $I,J$. For any
other pair (an ellipse and a line, say) the two non-finite B\'ezout points
are the conic's \emph{own} ideal pair, not the circular points; the
$\Iinfd$-contraction then strips the wrong direction and the resulting
grade-$2$ object is not the intersection. Outside the round sub-family, the

\paragraph{Relation to the pencil method.}
The blade $I_6$ is also the algebraic carrier of the classical \emph{pencil}
of conics through the four points~\cite{halsted_synthetic_1906}: the conics
$\lambda\,\mathbf{c}_1^{*}+\mu\,\mathbf{c}_2^{*}$ all contain the quadpole
$Q$, and locating a degenerate member ($\Delta_3=0$, a cubic in
$\lambda:\mu$ by \cref{eq:delta3-meet}) splits the intersection into
line--conic problems. This is the route developed
in~\cite{chomicki_intersection_2023}, which the shared-factor organisation
above complements: the pencil method computes the points; the infinity
signatures predict their finite/ideal budget in advance and identify when the
conformal shortcut applies, recovering the special configurations
of~\cite{byrtus_specific_2021} as instances of one rule.

\section{Conclusion and Future Work}
\label{sec:conclusion}
We have presented Conic Conformal Geometric Algebra as a single, consolidated
foundation for two-dimensional conic geometry: one algebra
($\mathbb{R}_{5,3}$), one null working basis with a small set of special
blades whose geometric roles --- size, anisotropy, tilt, origin and infinity
gauges --- organise everything that follows. On this base we assembled the
complete object taxonomy in both OPNS and IPNS form, structured by three
construction ladders (bare multipoles, the $\Iod$-gauged conic ladder, and
the CGA sub-array embedded by $\Iinfd$); closed-form, eigensolver-free
extraction of every classical conic feature from the dual vector by inner
products with the special blades; the explicit versors of the planar
similarities together with a sharp delimitation of the versor group ---
similarities on the full conic family, conformal maps on the round sub-family
only, and mere outermorphisms beyond; and a unified intersection calculus in
which the regressive product, one gauge-stripping contraction, and an
infinity-signature bookkeeping rule reduce every pairwise intersection to its
B\'ezout budget. Throughout, the boundary between algebra and arithmetic is
explicit and, we argue, optimal: every structural operation ---
construction, incidence, classification, feature extraction, intersection
reduction --- is geometric algebra, and the only numeric residue is root
extraction, which carries the irreducible algebraic content of the problem.

Three directions remain open. First, the lift to three dimensions: CCGA is
the planar restriction of QCGA
($\mathbb{R}_{9,6}$)~\cite{breuils_three-dimensional_2019}, and most of the
structure presented here --- the gauge blades, the trace--deviatoric split,
the shared-factor reduction --- has a natural quadric analogue whose
systematic development is future work. Second, a fully GA-native
factorisation of degenerate conics: splitting a line pair into its two lines
currently requires the adjugate of the coefficient matrix, and a blade-level
factorisation would close the last coordinate step in the intersection
pipeline. Third, the transformation group: shears and general projective
maps act on CCGA only as outermorphisms, and whether a larger algebra can
host them as versors --- as $\mathbb{R}_{4,4}$ does for projective maps of
points~\cite{goldman_r4_2015} --- while retaining the conic object calculus
is, to our knowledge, open.
